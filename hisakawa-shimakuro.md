# HISAKAWA SHIMAKURO
-# [久川 シマクロ] — The Steadfast Blade

### ⚔ GENERAL INFORMATION

- **Name:** Hisakawa Shimakuro  
- **Age:** 25 years old  
- **Gender:** Male  
- **Occupation:** Wandering Swordsman

### ⚔ ORIGINS & PERSONALITY

Shimakuro was raised in a quiet coastal village alongside his younger sister, **Shiina**. They spent much of their childhood wandering along the shore, watching fishing boats return to the harbor, and training under an elderly swordmaster.

Their mother's sister is the mother of **Shinohara Mei**, making Mei their maternal cousin.

Even as a child, Shimakuro was more reserved than his sister. He preferred observing his surroundings and listening to others rather than joining every conversation.

That habit has stayed with him. He is **quiet, disciplined, and careful with his words**, rarely speaking unless he has something worth saying.

Despite his serious appearance, he is not unfriendly. He treats others with patience and occasionally makes dry remarks that can catch people off guard.

Shiina is usually the first to notice when he is joking.

He cares deeply for his younger sister, although he is not particularly good at expressing it. Instead, he tends to show his concern through small gestures, often without drawing attention to them.

Her tendency to trust people too easily worries him, but he knows she would not appreciate being treated like a child.

### ⚔ THE MASTER'S TEACHINGS

Shimakuro was a dedicated student who took his training seriously. He respected his master greatly, though he often asked questions whenever a lesson did not make sense to him.

During a visit to a nearby settlement, he once witnessed a fisherman being falsely accused by a local official.

Angered by the situation, Shimakuro reached for his sword, but his master stopped him before he could draw it.

The old swordsman approached the official instead. After a lengthy discussion, the misunderstanding was resolved and the fisherman was allowed to leave.

Shimakuro later asked why his master had stopped him when the fisherman was clearly being treated unfairly.

His master answered:

> *"A blade may defend justice, but wisdom must decide when to draw it."*

Shimakuro never forgot that day.

He had been ready to fight without considering whether fighting was necessary in the first place.

Since then, he has tried to approach conflicts with a clearer mind. He is still willing to draw his sword when needed, but he does not believe every insult or injustice must be answered with violence.

### ⚔ HONOR & CONVICTION

Shimakuro takes his responsibilities seriously. If he pledges his sword to a province, he intends to honor that promise and serve it faithfully.

He respects the **Shogun** and understands why authority and order are necessary.

However, he does not believe that loyalty requires abandoning his own judgment.

If Shimakuro were ordered to harm innocent people or commit an act he considered unjust, he would refuse, even if the command came from the Shogun himself.

He would not make that choice without hesitation. He knows that disobeying a superior can have serious consequences, and he would first look for another way to resolve the situation.

But if he were left with no other option, **he would accept those consequences rather than carry out the order**.

His principles sometimes make him stubborn. Once he has carefully considered something and reached a decision, changing his mind can be difficult.

Shiina occasionally reminds him of this, much to his annoyance.

Although they approach certain matters differently, Shimakuro respects his sister's belief that people deserve a chance to explain themselves.

He sometimes worries that her kindness makes her vulnerable to manipulation, but he has never considered it a flaw.

If anything, he admires her ability to remain compassionate in situations where he might struggle to do the same.

They may disagree from time to time, but neither has ever felt the need to prove the other wrong simply for having a different perspective.

### ⚔ THE PLACE YOU RETURN TO

Shimakuro returns to his hometown whenever his travels allow it, though he does not always stay for very long.

The village remains familiar to him. He still recognizes the old paths leading down to the shore and the places where he and Shiina used to spend their afternoons as children.

On a hill overlooking the sea stands the grave of their former master.

Whenever he visits, Shimakuro makes sure the grave is properly cared for, clearing away fallen leaves and replacing any flowers that have withered.

A tree grows nearby, offering a quiet place to rest.

He often sits beneath it with his writing materials, practicing calligraphy while listening to the waves below.

There are days when he catches himself remembering something his master once said, or a lesson he had struggled to understand when he was younger.

He sometimes wonders what the old man would think of the swordsman he has become.

When Shiina is home, she usually comes to find him.

What begins as a peaceful afternoon often turns into a long conversation about their travels, their childhood, or whatever has been on her mind lately.

Shimakuro occasionally tells her that she talks too much, though the slight smile on his face usually gives away that he is only teasing.

He rarely admits how much he appreciates those moments.

For someone who spends so much of his life traveling, being able to return to a familiar place with his sister is a comfort he does not take for granted.

And whenever it is time to leave again, he makes sure to pay one last visit to their master's grave before setting off.
